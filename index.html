Key findings

The United States is 100% import-reliant for natural graphite, manganese, niobium, gallium, tantalum, titanium sponge and heavy rare earths, and the mines that supply them sit mostly in China, Brazil, Gabon and South Africa.
  1. Cobalt is the sharpest ownership concentration: CMOC's Tenke Fungurume and Kisanfu produced 114.2 kt in 2024, about 38% of world mine output, and CATL holds a further 23.75% of Kisanfu. US import reliance is 79%.
  2. Rare earths: China mined 69% of the world's rare earths in 2025 (270 of 390 kt REO, USGS), and Myanmar's militia-run Kachin mines feed Chinese separators. Mountain Pass, the only scaled US mine, supplied about 12% of 2024 output.
  3. Uranium: Chinese SOEs CGN and CNNC (about 15%) and Rosatom's Uranium One (about 10%) together controlled roughly a quarter of 2024 world production (WNA), and Niger's junta nationalised Orano's SOMAIR mine in 2025.
  4. Nickel: Indonesia supplied 67% of world nickel in 2025 (USGS), mostly through Chinese-built HPAL and smelting parks; Tsingshan-led Weda Bay alone is estimated at about 14% of world supply.
  5. Copper joined the critical list in 2025 and US import reliance rose to 57%. The largest copper mines are mostly Western-owned, but Chinese SOEs control Las Bambas and Tenke Fungurume and hold 39.6% of Kamoa-Kakula.
  6. Tungsten (79% Chinese), antimony (36% Chinese, plus Tajikistan and Russia) and graphite (78% Chinese) have almost no non-Chinese mine supply at scale; all three are DLA stockpile acquisition targets or under evaluation.

Methodology, precision and data gaps

Scope is the major producing mines, roughly the top five to ten per mineral by output, for the commodities that matter most to US supply chains, not every mine. Byproduct metals such as gallium, germanium and indium come from refineries rather than dedicated mines, so their sources appear as host mines (Huize, Kipushi, Red Dog, CBG, Boffa, Gejiu).

  • Ownership is equity % from company reports, ICSG and WNA; the owner HQ flag and control type follow the largest known equity holder.
  • State-linked means Chinese SOEs or groups with a state or SASAC anchor shareholder (CMOC, Zijin, China Minmetals/MMG, CGN, CNNC, Chalco, Pangang), Russian state or sanctioned strategic firms (Rosatom, Nornickel, Polyus, EVRAZ, Uralkali, UMMC), or other governments (Codelco, Gécamines, MIND ID, Kazatomprom, KGHM, Orano).
  • Production is the latest annual figure found, mostly calendar 2024; fiscal-year figures are labelled FY, and nickel mine figures are GlobalData estimates for 2023.
  • World share divides mine output by the USGS 2024 world total in the same unit (WNA 2024 for uranium); spodumene, LCE, ore tonnes and PGM ounces are not converted, so those mines show no share (n/a).
  • Equity-attributed share (chart builder) splits a mine's world share by equity %; where an owner's % is unpublished the unallocated remainder is split evenly among those owners.
  • Gaps: no credible mine-level 2024 output was found for El Teniente, Morenci, Oyu Tolgoi, Bayan Obo, the Chinese tungsten, antimony and graphite mines, most manganese, titanium and PGM operations, Rampura Agucha, Carajás and the potash mines; Chinese mine-level data is especially thin.
  • Not used: S&P Capital IQ and the IEA Critical Minerals Outlook (paywalled or country-level only); they could refine shares in a later update.

Coordinate precision

  • Datum WGS 84 (EPSG:4326). Coordinates are given to 4 decimal places; that is display precision, not accuracy. Use the precision class and circular error (CE) ring.
  • Map layers: the choropleth uses USGS MCS 2026 2025-estimate mine production by country (Pt+Pd for PGMs, ilmenite for titanium, usable ore for iron ore; US lithium and antimony are withheld; no uranium, which MCS does not cover). Arcs run from each foreign owner's HQ-country centroid to the mine (domestic owners are omitted); the heatmap is a kernel density of tracked mines weighted by world share.
  • MGRS is computed in-browser from WGS 84 (UTM zones with the Norway/Svalbard exceptions; polar regions n/a) to 1 m resolution.
  • Basemaps are live tiles: Esri World Imagery, Esri World Topographic, Esri Dark Gray Canvas, and Terrarium elevation tiles on AWS for hillshade and 3D terrain. Imagery dates vary by location; check the Esri metadata before using it for change detection.

About Quantum Xi

Quantum Xi (Quantum X Innovations Group) is a decision-support and geospatial-intelligence (GEOINT) firm. This atlas is a Quantum Xi open-source product: it fuses corporate ownership, production statistics, US criticality and stockpile status with verified mine locations, so analysts can see who controls the supply of strategic minerals and where.
